Who built this

Hey, I'm Gurneil.

I'm in grade ten, in the IB programme at Turner Fenton in Brampton, and I'm in HOSA — the future health professionals club. Last year I competed in CPR and Basic Life Support at two leadership conferences. You learn the protocol properly: check, call, compress. Every step is written down because under pressure people skip things.

Then I started reading about AI scribes — tools that listen to an appointment and write the note afterwards. They're already used on real patients, and the studies keep finding the same thing: the notes come out fluent, professional, and sometimes wrong. A dose nobody said. A "denies chest pain" that became "reports chest pain." One word, and the chart now says something the patient didn't.

What got me is that you can't catch that by reading harder. A wrong note doesn't look wrong — that's the whole problem. So I built the thing that checks, instead of the thing that writes.
