case_01_numeric_medication_error · Primary care, hypertension follow-up

Encounter transcript · source of truth

Doctor: Hi Mr. Alvarez, good to see you. How have you been feeling since we adjusted your blood pressure medication?

Patient: Pretty good, actually. No headaches or dizziness like before.

Doctor: That's great. Are you still taking the lisinopril 10 milligrams once a day?

Patient: Yes, every morning with breakfast.

Doctor: Any side effects, cough, swelling in your legs?

Patient: No, nothing like that.

Doctor: Let's check your blood pressure today… okay, it's reading 128 over 82. That's a nice improvement from last visit.

Patient: That's good to hear.

Doctor: I want you to keep taking the lisinopril at the same dose and come back in three months for a recheck.

Patient: Sounds good, thank you.

AI-drafted note · under test

SPatient reports feeling well since blood pressure medication adjustment.
SDenies headaches, dizziness, cough, or leg swelling.
STaking lisinopril 20mg once daily each morning with breakfast.
OBlood pressure 128/82, improved from previous visit.
AHypertension, well-controlled on current regimen.
PContinue lisinopril at current dose. Return to clinic in 3 months.

Nothing checked yet. Press Run the check to decompose the note and verify each claim against the transcript.

Single-prompt baseline, same note: it catches this one too — one call, one holistic read, correct answer. The gap shows up elsewhere. On case_16, a note missing a documented sulfa allergy with a prior hives reaction, the baseline reported zero issues: a whole-note free-form review has no mechanism that would surface something the note never says. That's what the dedicated omission node exists to close.

The assumption underneath everything

The transcript is
a model output too

Every node above checks the note against the transcript. That was fine while the transcript was typed by hand. The moment it comes out of a speech recogniser, this system is grounding itself in the one thing it never verified.

What the recogniser heard

“Hi, this is Alpera. I'm here for the studio… I think I've got a big one… Are you still taking a little bit of a 10-year-old? … It's leading 178.83. It's a nice and beautiful from last week.”

The same encounter as the demo above, through a bad speakerphone. Whisper didn't fail loudly — it returned fluent, grammatical English describing a conversation that never happened.

What the pipeline said about it

8 claims checked. All 8 supported.

And it was right to. Every claim genuinely is entailed by the transcript it was given. The note faithfully represents a fiction. When the transcript is the thing that's wrong, a perfect score is exactly what a correct implementation produces — a hallucinated encounter, certified accurate, ready to sign.

Node 3b withdrew all 8, each with the seconds of audio to re-listen to.

The threshold that catches it was measured, not guessed. One recording, degraded in steps:

clean0.835 – 0.901dose + BP intact
mild0.801 – 0.869dose + BP intact
heavy0.362 – 0.369both lost — invented speech
severe0.313 – 0.671both lost

Everything that kept the clinical facts scored at least 0.801. Everything that destroyed them scored at most 0.369. The cutoff sits at 0.55, in the gap — and the script that measured it ships with the number it produced.
